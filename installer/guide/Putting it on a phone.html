DCPutting it on a phone

District Nerve Center · Bajaur — one page, about fifteen seconds per handset

There is no app to download and nothing to install. The system is opened in the phone's browser and then added to the home screen, after which it opens like any other app — full screen, its own icon, no address bar.

This is worth doing on every handset, not just for tidiness. Once it is on the home screen, the app still opens when the phone has no signal at all, and an emergency can still be reported. The report waits on the handset and delivers itself the moment there is a connection, with nobody having to remember to re-send it. Opened as an ordinary web page instead, that does not work reliably.

Before you start

You need the district's own web address — the one that begins https://. It was set up when the district pointed its domain at the office machine. Get it from the office machine's Start Menu → District Nerve Center → Check the District Nerve Center, which prints it.

Write the address here, then pin this page up in the office

https://______________________________

It must be the https:// address, not a number like 192.168.1.40:3000.

This is not a preference. A phone will only keep an app working offline, and only give it the phone's location, when the address is a secure https:// one. Open it by IP address and it looks like it worked — the screens are all there — but the app will not open when there is no signal, and an emergency reported at a scene with no coverage may be lost. That is the one thing this system exists to prevent.

The address still points at the machine in this office. Nothing about the district's record leaves the building; the district's own internet line going down does not stop the control room working.

Do this on the phone

Android — Chrome

  1. Open Chrome.
  2. Type the address above and go to it.
  3. Tap the ⋮ menu, top right.
  4. Tap Add to Home screen (it may say Install app).
  5. Tap Install or Add.

The icon appears on the home screen. Open it from there from now on — not from Chrome.

iPhone — Safari

  1. Open Safari. It must be Safari; Chrome on iPhone cannot do this.
  2. Type the address above and go to it.
  3. Tap the Share button — the square with an arrow, at the bottom.
  4. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  5. Tap Add, top right.

The icon appears on the home screen. Open it from there from now on — not from Safari.

Signing in

Each officer signs in with their own mobile number and the password the administrator gave them. Everything they can see and do comes from the post they hold, not from the handset — so a shared phone is fine, and a handover takes effect immediately.

Sign out at the end of a shift on a shared handset. Whoever is signed in is who the record says filed the report. Leaving the previous officer signed in does not just look untidy — it puts their name against somebody else's work, permanently, in a record that cannot be edited afterwards.

Reporting an emergency

Open the app, tap Report, choose what kind and how bad, and submit. That is two taps and a button, and it is meant to take under fifteen seconds — if it is slower than making the phone call it replaces, it will lose to the phone call.

Where you are is attached automatically if the phone can work it out. Details — the place, a description — are asked for after the report is already safely recorded, never before. Nothing waits on you finishing them.

When there is no signal

Report exactly as normal. The app says No connection and holds the report on the handset. When a connection returns it delivers itself, with no action from you and nothing to remember. The system records both when the emergency happened and when it arrived, so a report held for six hours is not mistaken for one that came in six hours late.

An emergency can be recorded even when nobody is signed in — a duty officer whose session expired overnight in a place with no signal cannot sign in, and losing that report would be worse than not knowing who typed it.
