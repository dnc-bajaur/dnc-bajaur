# The Connectivity Ladder

The single most important design artifact for this district.

Connectivity in Bajaur is not binary. There is routine poor coverage, congested links,
data-down-but-voice-up, and deliberate shutdowns during security operations. Each of the
five levels below is a **distinct product state** with its own guarantees, its own screen,
and its own written operating procedure.

**The system always announces which rung it is on. It never pretends to be on a higher one.**

---

## L0 — Normal

*Full connectivity.*

Live push to central and department boards. Notifications delivered and confirmed. SLA
timers running server-side. Everything the product promises.

- **Degrades on:** sustained push failure or heartbeat loss drops the affected client to L1.
- **Screen:** normal operation, no banner.

---

## L1 — Degraded

*Intermittent or slow link.*

Realtime push falls back to interval polling. Writes still reach the server, but with
latency. The board carries a persistent banner naming the delay, and freshness badges
tighten.

- **Lost:** sub-second awareness.
- **Held:** durability, ordering, SLA correctness.
- **Screen:** persistent banner — *"Live updates delayed. Last sync 47s ago."*

---

## L2 — Store and forward

*Server unreachable, device working.*

The client keeps functioning against local state. New reports, acknowledgements and
response actions are written to the local outbox with `occurred_at` stamped at the device.

Each queued item shows an explicit **pending sync** state — never a checkmark. A user must
never believe an emergency has reached the control room when it has not left the handset.

On reconnect, events replay in order with idempotency keys (see INV-08).

- **Lost:** central visibility, notification dispatch, cross-department awareness.
- **Held:** the record. Nothing entered is discarded.
- **Screen:** offline banner, per-item pending badges, visible outbox count with a manual
  retry.

---

## L3 — Out of band

*Data network down, voice and SMS alive.*

Field reporting shifts to SMS and phone. An SMS gateway parses a minimal structured format
into the same `Report` entity. Calls are entered by a control-room operator on a wired
connection. Critical notifications go out as SMS and voice.

The district is still coordinating — through a narrower pipe into the same record.

- **Lost:** media attachments, precise GPS, self-service department workspaces.
- **Held:** intake, routing, central command view, escalation.
- **Screen:** control room operates normally; field users are on SMS. Board flags which
  incidents arrived out-of-band.

### SMS format

Kept short enough to be memorised and dictated over radio. Free text after the fixed
prefix is accepted and enriched later by an operator.

```
EMG <category> <place-code> <severity> <free text>
```

Anything unparseable is still ingested as a raw report for operator triage — an SMS is
never rejected for format (INV-01).

---

## L4 — Blackout

*Nothing digital reaches the district.*

The system's job at this level is not to run. It is to **have prepared for its own absence.**

A printed duty card and a numbered paper log form — generated by the platform and kept
current at every station — carry the same fields as the intake screen. Radio and runners
carry the traffic.

When service returns, a dedicated **catch-up screen** ingests the paper log with real
`occurred_at` times, flags the entries as retrospectively recorded, and reconstructs the
timeline honestly.

- **Lost:** everything automated.
- **Held:** the district still has one shape of record, and the history is recoverable
  rather than simply missing.
- **Deliverable:** the printed forms and the SOP are project artifacts, produced in M3 —
  not improvised during the first blackout.

---

## The timestamp problem

An incident reported at 14:02 during an outage may reach the server at 16:40.

If SLA logic naively uses receipt time, the response looks instantaneous and the metrics
lie. If it naively uses report time, a reconnect fires two hours of retroactive
escalations at once. Neither is acceptable, so the rule is explicit:

| Concern | Uses | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Measurement and reporting** | `occurred_at` | Tells the truth: this took 2h 41m, and here is why |
| **Escalation firing** | `recorded_at` + grace window | A reconnect produces one labelled *late arrival* alert per incident, not a retroactive storm |
| **Ordering within an incident** | `occurred_at`, tie-broken by `recorded_at` then event id | Deterministic replay |
| **Operational risk surfacing** | the gap between them | This is the district's real connectivity picture, measured rather than assumed |

**The gap is a first-class field.** Any incident where `recorded_at - occurred_at` exceeds
a threshold is tagged and surfaced on the central board — because that gap is precisely
the operational risk the DC needs to see.

---

## Detection and transition

Rung is determined per client, not globally, and transitions are hysteretic — a single
failed request does not drop a client to L2, and a single success does not restore L0.

- **L0 → L1:** push channel dead or round-trip latency over threshold, sustained.
- **L1 → L2:** writes failing past retry budget.
- **L2 → L1/L0:** successful sync, outbox drained, push re-established.
- **L3 and L4** are declared operationally by the control room, not detected — because a
  device cannot distinguish "the network is down" from "the network is down everywhere."
  Declaring the rung switches procedures and is itself a logged event.

---

## Testing requirement

**Airplane mode is a required test environment, not an optional one.**

Every milestone gate that touches a write path includes an offline test. M3's gate is a
staged blackout drill — the network pulled for two hours — which must end with zero lost
incidents, honest timestamps, and no notification storm on recovery.
